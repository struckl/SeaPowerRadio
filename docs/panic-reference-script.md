# Panic reference recording: what the speaker says (about 2 minutes)

This is the short way of option 1 (see `panic-recording.md` for recording every line). One person speaks about two
minutes of panic. We convert it into each R.A.T.S. Radio voice and use it as that voice's sample clip, so the voice
model speaks **any** line with that panic. The exact words do not matter. What matters:

- **Real fear, not radio voice.** You are hit, burning, sinking, about to die. Shout, gasp, break off, repeat yourself.
- **Variety.** High and low, loud and choked, fast and stumbling, so the model hears the whole range.
- **Many different sounds of English.** The text below is written to cover them; reading it as written is fine.
- **Keep going.** Do not stop between blocks; out of breath is good. Two or three full runs, the best one is used.

Setup and permission are the same as in `panic-recording.md`: phone at arm's length, quiet room, 30 seconds of
jumping jacks first, and the one-line CC0 permission. One man and one woman, each the whole script.

## The script

Read straight through. The words in *italics* are directions, not to be spoken.

**1. Hit (about 20 s)** *a second ago something hit you; shocked, shouting*

> I'm hit! I'm hit! Something hit the tail, I've got no control, the stick is dead! Warning lights everywhere, everything's
> red! Jesus, I can't hold her, she's rolling, she's rolling!

**2. Fire (about 20 s)** *smoke in the cockpit, coughing is good*

> Fire! Fire in the cockpit! I can't see, there's smoke everywhere, I can't breathe! It's getting hot, my hands, my
> hands are burning! Mayday, mayday, mayday, this is Viper two-one, I'm on fire, I'm going down!

**3. Trapped (about 20 s)** *the panic rises, voice cracks, begging*

> The canopy won't open! It's stuck, it's stuck, I can't get out! Come on, come on, open! Please, please! Somebody,
> anybody, help me! I don't want to die out here, not like this!

**4. Under attack (about 20 s)** *straining in a hard turn, short words between grunts*

> Missile, missile, it's on me! Breaking left! Come on... come on! It's still there, I can't shake it! Flares, flares!
> Where is it? Where is it? Oh God, it's right behind me!

**5. The ship (about 20 s)** *shouting over alarms and water*

> We're hit, we're hit! Torpedo, starboard side! We're taking water, the engine room is flooding! Get them out, get
> everybody out! She's listing, she's going over! Abandon ship! Abandon ship!

**6. The end (about 15 s)** *quieter, breathless, scared, almost crying; then one last shout*

> I can't... I can't hold on much longer. Tell them... tell them I tried. Oh God. Oh God, here it comes!

**7. Sounds (about 10 s)** *no words, each one twice*

A sharp gasp. A groan of strain, pulling hard. Heavy breathing for three breaths. A short cry, "argh!". A cough.

## Other languages later

The same seven blocks, translated with the same feelings, spoken by a native speaker: the converter only needs the
panic, not English. One person per language and gender is enough for a whole voice pack.
